# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Companies hiring (primary):** founders, CTOs and product leads who need a team to build, scale or rescue their software. They land on the site to judge whether OSS House can be trusted with their product, and the action is getting in touch.
- **Developers (secondary):** people who use, star or contribute to OSS House's open-source projects. The projects double as proof of craft for the first group.

## Product Purpose

OSS House is a software house ("Open Source Software House"; the expansion is a working name, not yet confirmed as public copy). It does two things under one roof:

- client work: designing and building software for other companies;
- an in-house lab of open-source products it builds and maintains itself.

The landing page succeeds when a company decides to contact OSS House, with the open-source work as the evidence that tipped the decision.

## Positioning

Most software houses ask to be trusted on screenshots and logos. OSS House's work is in the open: a prospect can read the code, the commits and the maintained projects before hiring. Client work and open-source work feed each other.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Ships in English and Brazilian Portuguese (en + pt).
- Light and dark themes for now; more may come.
- Open decisions: services list, case studies, team, which open-source projects are listed as OSS House's, contact channel.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: "OSS House", written in lowercase as "oss house" in the logo.
- Logo: `public/logo/oss-house.svg`, the letters "oss" drawn in one round stroke, where the "o" is a coiled snake with its head at the end of the stroke. The mark is to be refined, not replaced.
- Logo motion the owner asked for: the "o" grows from a dot into a circle and then into the snake's head; the two "s" letters get their own motion; on scroll the "s" letters leave to the left and the snake rotates, so the header carries only the snake.

## Evidence on Hand

- The logo SVG above.
- No case studies, client names, testimonials, metrics or project list yet. Do not invent any of them; label demonstration content as synthetic.

## Product Principles

1. Show the work, don't describe it: real code and real projects outrank claims.
2. Companies first, developers close behind: every page leads to contact, with open source as the proof.
3. Bilingual by default: no string ships in one language only.
4. Craft is the pitch: the site itself is a sample of the work.
